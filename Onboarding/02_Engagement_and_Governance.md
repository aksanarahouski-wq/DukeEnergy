## The Contract in Plain Language

We are operating under **Statement of Work #1 (SOW #1)** between Orases and Duke Energy Business Services LLC, effective February 2026 and running through **December 31, 2027**.

This is a **Time and Materials (T&M) engagement**. That means:
- We bill for actual hours worked at a blended rate
- There is no fixed-fee guarantee — the more hours we work, the more Duke pays
- There is a non-binding total estimate, but it is not a cap
- Every piece of work must be approved in writing before we execute it

This model gives Duke flexibility to reprioritize at any time. It also means **scope discipline is our responsibility**. Every informal ask, every verbal change, every "while you're in there" request is real money if we execute it without a formal approval.

## How Work Gets Authorized

This is the most important process to internalize. **No work begins without written client approval of the estimate.**

The flow every sprint:

```
PM/BA drafts estimate for next sprint or work package
        ↓
PM submits written estimate to Duke's Product Owner
        ↓
Duke Product Owner approves in writing (email counts)
        ↓
Work begins
        ↓
Sprint status report delivered to Duke at end of every sprint
```

If this gate gets skipped — even once, even for something small — we have no contractual protection if Duke disputes the work. This is not bureaucracy; it is how we get paid and how we protect the team.

## The Acceptance Process

When we deliver a sprint or a formal deliverable, the clock starts:

- Duke has **10 business days** to accept or reject in writing
- No response within 10 days = deemed accepted (this protects us)
- If rejected, we have **10 business days** to fix or deliver a response plan
- Duke then has another **10 business days** to accept or reject again

**Deliver in writing every time.** A Confluence page or an email with a link — whatever channel, it needs a clear delivery date on record. Do not rely on verbal delivery in a meeting.

## What Duke Owes Us

The SOW explicitly lists Duke's obligations. These are your dependency list — if Duke falls behind on any of these, document it immediately in writing:

- Maintain a single empowered Product Owner
- Attend all scheduled meetings
- Provide timely written approvals on estimates and deliverables
- Give us access to existing systems, APIs, and infrastructure
- Provide access to subject matter experts (SMEs) for requirements validation — **10–15 hours per week**
- Complete UAT and provide formal written acceptance within the review window
- Notify us in advance of stakeholder changes, leave, or business case changes

If Duke delays and it causes our schedule to slip, that delay is CLIENT-caused. That protects our timeline commitments — but only if we documented the delay at the time it happened.

## Change Orders — When We Need One

A Change Order (signed by both parties) is required when:
- Duke requests scope outside the approved backlog
- A Key Milestone will slip more than 10 business days
- We need to expand team size or significantly change resourcing

For smaller changes: the PM provides written impact (timeline, scope) and gets written approval before we touch it. Every informal request that gets executed without approval is risk we are absorbing alone.

## Glossary of Abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Full Term |
|---|---|
| **BA** | Business Analyst |
| **MVP** | Minimum Viable Product — the core version of the app, targeting September 30, 2026 (subject to alignment with Duke) |
| **PM** | Product Manager (Aksana Rahouski, Orases) — unless context specifies "Project Manager" |
| **PRD** | Product Requirements Document — defines what we are building and why |

| **T&M** | Time and Materials — the billing model; we bill for actual hours worked at $250/hour blended rate |
| **TRD** | Technical Requirements Document — defines how we will build what the PRD describes |
| **UAT** | User Acceptance Testing — formal testing conducted by Duke to verify delivered features meet requirements before acceptance |
| **UX/UI** | User Experience / User Interface — the design discipline covering how the product looks and how users interact with it |
