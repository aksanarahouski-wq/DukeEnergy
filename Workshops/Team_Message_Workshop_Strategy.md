## The Opportunity

Duke Energy has come back and requested a **4-session discovery workshop series** to dive deep into the MVP and Phase 1 scope, with the goal of creating wireframe samples by the end. This is a critical opportunity to close this deal, but we need to be strategic about how we use these sessions.

## The Reality Check

Before we get excited about creating beautiful wireframes, we need to acknowledge what we **don't know yet**:

### **We're Not Building Just One App - We're Building an Ecosystem**

This isn't simply a customer-facing mobile app. We're building an interconnected ecosystem with at least **three distinct user groups**:

1. **Customers** - requesting services, managing home inventory, accessing DIY content
2. **Admins** - managing product catalog, pricing, content, service operations
3. **Contractors** - receiving job assignments, updating status, communicating with customers

Each of these needs different interfaces, workflows, and data access patterns.

### **Critical Unknowns That Will Impact Everything**

We've made a lot of assumptions in the proposal, but we need to validate the fundamentals:

- **User Groups:** Are our personas accurate? What's the actual distribution of customer types?
- **Existing Data:** What customer/contractor/service data already exists in Duke's systems?
- **Business Processes:** How does service request/contractor assignment actually work today?
- **Integration Reality:** What APIs exist vs. what Duke IT needs to build?
- **Data We're Building:** Home inventory, DIY content, product catalog - what's the strategy?

**If we get these wrong, we could design the wrong solution and lose credibility.**

---

## Our Strategic Approach: Validate Before We Design

I've restructured the workshop plan to prioritize **discovery and validation** over assumptions. Here's the strategy:

### **Session 1: Foundation Validation & Data Discovery**
**Goal:** VALIDATE everything we think we know

**Focus Areas:**
- **VALIDATE foundational assumptions** about user groups, personas, and business processes
  - Are our 3 personas real? What's the actual customer distribution?
  - What are the REAL pain points (not our assumptions)?

- **DISCOVER what data exists** in current Duke Energy systems vs. what needs to be built
  - What's in Commerce? What's in Dynamics? What contractor data exists?
  - Where are the data silos? What's the master source of truth?

**Output:** System architecture map, data source matrix, validated personas
**Wireframes:** Simple sketches only - NO detailed wireframes yet!

---

### **Session 2: Service Request Process & Business Rules**
**Goal:** Understand the ACTUAL process, not our assumptions

**Focus Areas:**
- **Walk through REAL service requests** from Duke's systems
  - Show us how it works TODAY (screen recordings, actual data)
  - HPP covered service, ad-hoc service, emergency service, denied service

- **CLARIFY technical integration** requirements and data availability
  - What Dynamics APIs exist RIGHT NOW?
  - What needs to be built by Duke IT?
  - What's the timeline for new APIs?

- **DEFINE business rules** that drive the system
  - HPP coverage determination (who decides, what rules?)
  - Contractor matching logic (automated, manual, hybrid?)
  - Service prioritization and SLAs

**Output:** Process flow diagrams, business rules documentation, integration requirements
**Wireframes:** Conceptual flows only - wait for validation!

---

### **Session 3: Home Inventory, Content & New Data Models**
**Goal:** DEFINE what we're building from scratch with clear rationale

**Focus Areas:**
- **Start with "WHY"** for each feature
  - Why do we need home inventory? What problem does it solve?
  - What's the minimum viable version?
  - Is this MVP or Phase 2?

- **DEFINE user flows** based on validated requirements and data sources
  - What data do we actually need to track?
  - How does data flow between systems?
  - What's the content strategy for DIY library?

- **Scope reduction decisions**
  - What features can we defer to Phase 2?
  - What's essential vs. nice-to-have?

**Output:** Data models, content strategy, scope decisions (MVP vs. Phase 2)
**Wireframes:** NOW we can build detailed wireframes based on validated needs

---

### **Session 4: Consolidation, Integration Specs & Go-Forward Plan**
**Goal:** FINALIZE scope and get concrete on what happens next

**Focus Areas:**
- **Review and consolidate** Sessions 1-3 learnings
  - What did we validate? What did we defer?
  - What open questions remain?

- **Get concrete on integrations**
  - Specific API endpoints that exist TODAY
  - APIs Duke IT needs to build (timeline, owners)
  - Security, compliance, infrastructure decisions

- **BUILD shared understanding** of MVP scope and priorities
  - Final scope confirmation
  - Go-live planning and phased rollout
  - Next steps and milestones

**Output:** FINAL validated scope, integration specs, go-forward plan
**After Session 4:** Create comprehensive wireframe package (2-3 weeks)

## Why This Approach Wins the Deal

### **1. We Demonstrate Strategic Thinking**
Instead of jumping to wireframes, we're showing Duke that we:
- Understand the complexity of their ecosystem
- Ask the right questions before making assumptions
- Validate requirements before designing solutions
- Think about integrations, data, and business processes - not just UI

### **2. We De-Risk the Project**
By validating assumptions early, we:
- Avoid designing the wrong solution
- Identify integration blockers before development
- Right-size the scope (defer non-essential features)
- Build confidence that we understand their business

### **3. We Create True Partnership**
This approach shows we're:
- Collaborative, not prescriptive
- Interested in their reality, not our assumptions
- Building FOR them, not AT them
- Invested in their success, not just delivering a project

### **4. We Deliver Better Wireframes**
When we finally create wireframes after Session 4, they'll be:
- Based on validated user needs and business processes
- Informed by real data architecture and integration constraints
- Scoped to actual MVP (not fantasy features)
- Ready for development because requirements are solid

## What We Need from the Team

### **Before Session 1:**
- Review the workshop plan document I've created
- Prepare questions specific to your area (design, dev, BA, PM)
- Think about what unknowns would derail your work
- Come ready to listen and learn (not pitch solutions)

### **During Workshops:**
- **Listen more than we talk**
- Ask "show us how it works today" not "here's how we'd build it"
- Document everything (whiteboard, screenshots, process flows)
- Capture open questions and assign owners
- Don't promise features/timelines until we validate requirements

### **After Each Session:**
- Debrief as a team (what did we learn? what changed?)
- Update assumptions and risks
- Prepare for next session based on learnings
- Create simple concept sketches (not detailed wireframes yet)
